1.1 · Construct validityTheory~1:30

Validity: are we testing the right thing?

A test is valid if it measures accurately what it is intended to measure.

Construct validity: whether a test measures the underlying ability or trait (the construct) it claims to measure.

Two threats · Messick (1996)

Construct under-representation: the test is too narrow and misses important dimensions of the construct.

Construct-irrelevant variance: the test involves factors outside the construct, e.g. general knowledge, first-language background.

  • Vòng trái = năng lực cần đo; vòng phải = thứ bài test thực sự đo.
  • Chỉ vòng trái = under-representation; chỉ vòng phải = irrelevant variance. Slide sau: lớp tự nhận diện.

1.2.1 · Content validityTheory~1:00

Forms of validity evidence: content validity

Established when test content is a representative sample of the language skills, structures or objectives it is meant to cover.

  • Requires explicit test specifications, written before the test, to guide principled selection of items.
  • Prevents harmful backwash: skills the test ignores tend to be ignored in teaching too.
Picture it

Items spread across every unit, not bunched in one.

  • Nhấn mạnh: cần bảng đặc tả (test specs) trước khi viết đề.
  • Hình: chấm vàng = câu hỏi; bên trái phân bố đều, bên phải dồn vào 1 unit.

1.2.2–1.2.3 · Criterion-relatedTheory + example~1:00

Do scores agree with an independent criterion?

Concurrent validity

Test scores compared with a criterion given at roughly the same time.

High agreement = evidence of concurrent validity.

Predictive validity

How well a test predicts future performance.

Placement score predicts appropriate class placement.

Quick check: entrance English scores checked against results in English-medium courses a year later.
1.2.3 Other evidence: what test-takers actually do, via think-aloud or retrospection.
  • Concurrent: bài 10 phút so với bài 45 phút cùng thời điểm. Predictive: dự đoán kết quả tương lai.

1.3 · Scoring, face validity, fairnessTheory~1:00

Three more conditions for a valid test

Scoring validity

Score what you test

The scoring criteria should reflect the ability the test is intended to measure.

Face validity

Looks the part

The test looks as if it measures what it claims to. Looking right is not the same as construct validity.

Fairness

Design + administration

  • Equal chance to show ability: familiarisation, model versions, feedback, accommodations
  • Appropriate conditions, good equipment
  • Use tests only for their intended purpose
  • Đọc nhanh 3 khái niệm; slide sau là ví dụ để lớp gọi tên.

2 · ReliabilityTheory~1:00

Reliability: can we trust the score?

Reliability is the consistency of test scores across different test administrations or occasions.

Two sources of inconsistency:

1 · Candidates’ performance

Mood, health, sleep, luck with particular items.

2 · Scoring

Different scorers, or one scorer on different days.

  • Định nghĩa + 2 nguồn gây thiếu ổn định: thí sinh và người chấm.
2.1.1 · Reliability coefficientTheory → Rank~1:00

The reliability coefficient: how high is enough?

It depends on the stakes: the more important the decision, the higher the reliability we must demand. Rank from lowest to highest need:

B → A → D → C. A and B are both low stakes and can swap.

2.2 · SEMTheory → Play~1:00

Standard error of measurement: Lan’s 7.5 is a range

True score: the average a candidate would get if tested endlessly, with no learning or fatigue.

observed = true score + error

SEM estimates the uncertainty about the observed score.

  • Kéo SEM: nhỏ → dải hẹp; lớn → không chắc Lan đã vượt 7.0.
  • Chính xác: observed = true + error; SEM là độ lớn điển hình của sai số. ±1 SEM ≈ 68%, ±2 SEM ≈ 95%.
2.3 · Scorer reliabilityTheory~0:30

Would the same essay get the same mark?

Intra-scorer

Same scorer, same score on two occasions

Inter-scorer

Any other scorer gives that same score

Objective test

One easily recognised correct answer (MC). A computer could score it: scorer reliability = 1.

Subjective test

Interviews, essays need judgement. Not perfect, but can be made high enough.

  • Lướt nhanh, dẫn vào hoạt động "Candidate or Scorer?".
2.4 · More reliable testsTeam game~1:00

Candidate side (A) or scorer side (B)?

Take enough samples of behaviour
Provide a detailed scoring key
Write unambiguous items
Train scorers
Agree acceptable responses in advance
Clear, explicit instructions
Familiarise candidates with the format
Use multiple independent scorers

Key takeaway: reliability = (1) consistent candidate performance + (2) consistent scoring.

The complete picture: 14 strategies

A · Candidate / test side
  1. Take enough samples of behaviour
  2. Exclude items that don’t discriminate weaker from stronger
  3. Don’t allow candidates too much freedom
  4. Write unambiguous items
  5. Provide clear, explicit instructions
  6. Well laid out, perfectly legible tests
  7. Familiarise candidates with format and techniques
B · Scorer side
  1. Items that allow scoring as objective as possible
  2. Make comparisons between candidates direct
  3. Provide a detailed scoring key
  4. Train scorers
  5. Agree acceptable responses and scores beforehand
  6. Identify candidates by number, not name
  7. Employ multiple independent scorers
  • Hai đội luân phiên chọn A/B; bấm Check.
  • Sau đó mở "Show all 14" để xem toàn bộ, không giải thích từng cái.

3.1 · Positive & negative washbackTheory~1:00

Washback: what does the test make people do?

Washback (backwash) is the effect of testing on teaching and learning.

Positive (beneficial)

Preparing for the test means developing the abilities the course aims at.

Negative (harmful)

Test content or techniques differ from course objectives, so teaching narrows to the test.

  • Định nghĩa (Hughes & Hughes, 2020). Tích cực: ôn thi = phát triển đúng năng lực mục tiêu. Tiêu cực: nội dung/kỹ thuật thi lệch mục tiêu → dạy học thu hẹp theo đề.
